Approximations based on the normal distribution#

Non-central chi-squared distribution: cdf and sf (Penev)#

ctx.chi2_nc_penev_cdf(x, nu, nc)#

where ctx is fpm, mpm, ipm, dec, gmp or apm.

Returns an approximation to the cdf of the (non-)central chi-squared distribution.

[Penev2000` give the following first and second order Wiener germ approximation:

\[F_{\chi^2}\left(n, x; \lambda\right) \thickapprox \Phi \left(\text{sgn}(s) \sqrt{n(s-1)^2(1/(2s) + m- h(1-s)/s) - \log(A(s)) + 2B(s)/n} \right)\]
\[\text{where } m = \lambda/n; \quad h(y) = \frac{(1-y) \log(1-y)+y- \tfrac{1}{2}y^2}{y^2} ; \quad s= \frac{\sqrt{1+4xm/n}-1}{2m}\]
\[A(s) = \frac{1}{s} - \frac{2}{s} \cdot \frac{h(1-s)}{1+2ms}; \quad B(s) = \frac{(1+3m)^2}{9(1+2m)^3}\]

where \(\Phi(\cdot)\) denotes the cdf of the normal distribution (see ).

An example (CDF):

>>> from mpfunlab import fpm, mpm
>>> mpm.dps = 40; x = 12; nu = 10; nc = 30
>>> dx = dec.cdisn_penev(x, nu, nc); mx = mpm.cdisn_penev(x, nu, nc)
>>> ix = ipm.cdisn_penev(x, nu, nc); fx = fpm.cdisn_penev(x, nu, nc)
>>> gx = gmp.cdisn_penev(x, nu, nc); ax = apm.cdisn_penev(x, nu, nc)
>>> mpm.show([dx, mx, ix, fx, gx, ax])

(Non-central) chi-squared distribution: qtf and isf (Canal)#

ctx.chi2_nc_canal_qtf(LeftTail, RightTail, n)#

where ctx is fpm, mpm, ipm, dec, gmp or apm.

Returns an approximation to the quantile of the (non-)central chi-squared distribution.

Let \(z_\alpha\) and \(\chi^2_{n,\alpha}\) be the \(\alpha\)-quantiles of the standard normal distribution and central chi-square distribution with \(n\) the degrees of freedom. For \(n=1\) and \(n=2\), the following closed form expressions can be used:

\[\chi^2_{1,\alpha} = z^2_{\alpha}, \quad \chi^2_{2,\alpha} = 2 \log(1 - \alpha)\]

At the extreme left tail of the distribution, for small \(x\), the CDF of a \(\chi^2\) variable with \(n\) degrees of freedom can be approximated by the density of a \(\chi^2\) variable with \(n+2\) degrees of freedom:

\[F_{\chi^2}(n,x) \thickapprox 2 f_{\chi^2}(n+2,x).\]

The density of a \(\chi^2\) variable with \(n+2\) degrees of freedom can be inverted in closed form using the Lambert \(W\) function, which leads to the following approximation:

\[\chi^2_{n,\alpha} \thickapprox f^{-1}_{\chi^2}(n+2,\alpha) = -2 W(t)/a , \quad \text{where}\]
\[a=\frac{1}{(n+2)/2-1}, \quad k=\log(\Gamma((n+2)/2), \quad d=a-\log(1-\alpha)+k, \quad t=-a e^{p+d}\]

This approximation is used for \(|t|<0.1\), and the Lambert \(W\) function is approximated as

\[W(x) \thickapprox x - x^2 + \tfrac{3}{2} x^3 - \tfrac{8}{3} x^4 - \tfrac{125}{24} x^5.\]

Otherwise, the quantile is approximated by inverting a formula proposed by Canal [177]:

\[\chi^2_{n,\alpha} \thickapprox n\left( \frac{1}{2}+ \frac{t}{2}- \frac{3}{2t}\right)^6, \quad \text{where}\]
\[t = \left({-5+2L + 2 \sqrt{13-5L+L^2}} \right)^{1/3} , \quad L = 6 \left(m + s \left(z_{\alpha} + a (z^2_{\alpha} - 1) - a^2 (2 z^3_{\alpha} - 5 z_{\alpha}) \right) \right)\]
\[m = \frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{9n} - \frac{7}{648n^2} - \frac{25}{2187n^3}, \quad s^2 = \frac{1}{18n} + \frac{1}{162n^2} - \frac{37}{11664n^3}, \quad a = \frac{1}{162 \sqrt{2n^3}} = \gamma_1/6.\]

The noncentral quantile is approximated as

\[\chi^2_{n,\lambda,\alpha} \thickapprox (1+b) \chi^2_{n_1,\alpha} , \quad \text{where } n_1= \frac{(n+\lambda)^2}{n+2\lambda} , \quad b = \frac{\lambda}{n+\lambda}\]

An example (CDF):

>>> from mpfunlab import fpm, mpm
>>> mpm.dps = 40; x = 12; nu = 10; nc = 30

and \(\Phi(\cdot)\) and \(\phi(\cdot)\) denote the cdf and pdf of the normal distribution, respectively.

where ctx is fpm, mpm, ipm, dec, gmp or apm.

Returns an approximation to the qtf of the doubly noncentral t distribution, as decribed in Broda and Paolella [167].

Broda_2007_WorkingPaper proposes the following approximation:

\[F_{t''}(t;n;\mu,\theta) \approx F_{t'}(t/c;f;\mu), \quad \text{where}\]
\[f=\frac{7}{2} \left(-1+\sqrt{15-7g^2/h}\right)^{-1}, \quad c=\sqrt{h(1-2/f)}, \quad g=m_1/\sqrt{\mu^2/2}, h=m_2/(1+\mu^2),\]
\[m_1 = \mu \binom{n}{2}^{1/2} \frac{\Gamma((n-1)/2)}{\Gamma(n/2)} {}_1F_1 \left(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{n}{2}, -\frac{\theta}{2} \right)\]
\[m_2 = (1+\mu^2) \frac{n}{n-2} {}_1F_1 \left(\frac{2}{2}, \frac{n}{2}, -\frac{\theta}{2} \right)\]

Spearman’s \(\rho\) is calculated like Pearsons’s correlation coefficient, using the rank-transform on the \(x_i\) and \(y_i\). The exact test for \(H_0\) is the permutation test. Techniques for obtaining the exact distribution and approximations are given below

Mann-Whitney U distribution: general alternatives specified by rank order probabilities (Sundrum)#

ctx.mannwhitney_nc_mu4(N)#

where ctx is dec, mpm, fpm, gmp or apm.

Returns an approximation to the noncentral distribution of Mann-Whitney’s \(U\), based on the first 4 moments.

The first 4 moments under the alternative are given by Sundrum [536] (note that \(\kappa_4 = \mu_4 - 3\mu2^3\)):

\[\mu_1 = p\]
\[\mu_2 = (p^2 + p - q - r)/mn + (q - p^2 )/n + (r - p^2 )/m + p^2\]
\begin{eqnarray} \mu_3 & = & 6(p^3 + u - pq - pr)/mn + (2p^3 + s - 3pq)/n^2 + (2p^3 + t - 3pr)/m^2 \\ \nonumber & + & 3(3pq + 2(pr - u - p^3) + q - s - p^2)/mn^2 + 3(3pr + 2(pq - u - p^3) \\ \nonumber & + & r - t - p^2)/m^2n +(4p^3 + 3p^2 + p + 6u + 2(s + t) - 3(q + r)(1 + 2p))/m^2n^2 \nonumber \end{eqnarray}
\begin{eqnarray} \mu_4 & = & 3(q - p^2)2/n^2 + 6(q - p^2)(r - p^2)/mn + 3(r - p^2)2/m^2 \\ \nonumber & + & (12qp^2 + a - 4sp - 3q^2 - 6p^4)/n^3 + (12rp^2 + b - 4tp - 3r2 - 6p^4)/m^3 \\ \nonumber & + & 6(7(rp^2 - p^4) + 12qp^2 + qp + 2(w-sp) - 3(q^2 + qr) - 8up - p^3)/mn^2 \\ \nonumber & + & (42qp^2 + 72rp^2 + 6rp+ 12x - 42p^4 - 18r2 - 18qr - 12tp - 48up - 6p^3)/m^2n \\ \nonumber & + & 6(6(p^4 - rp^2) + 3(q^2 - qp) - 12qp^2 + 4sp + 8up + 2(p^3 + qr - w) + s - a)/mn^3 \\ \nonumber & + & (36p^4 + 18r2 + 12qr - 72rp^2 - 36qp^2 + 24tp - 6b + 48up \\ \nonumber & - & 12x + 12p^3 - 18rp+ 6t)/m^3 n \\ \nonumber & + & (105p^4 + 42p^3 + 3p^2 + 33q^2 + 33r2 + 54qr - 174qp^2 - 174rp^2 - 42pq \\ \nonumber & - & 42pr + 36sp + 36tp + 192up - 36w - 36x + 6v + 36u)/ m^2n^2 \\ \nonumber & + & (132qp^2 + 108rp^2 - 66p^4 - 33q^2 - 36qr - 18r2 - 44sp - 24tp + 11a \\ \nonumber & - & 144up + 36w + 24x - 6v - 36p^3 - 36u - 7p^2 + 54pq + 36pr - 18s + 7q)/m^2n^3 \\ \nonumber & + & (132rp^2 + 108qp^2 - 66p^4 - 33r2 - 36qr - 18q^2 - 44tp - 24sp + 11b \\ \nonumber & - & 144up + 24w + 36x - 6v - 36p^3 - 36u - 7p^2 + 54pr + 36pq - 18t + 7r)/m^3n^2 \\ \nonumber & + & (6(3(q^2 + r2)- 12((q + r)p^2) + 4(p^3 + qr + sp + tp - w - x) - (a + b - v) + 16up \\ \nonumber & - & 6(p^4 + pq + pr + u) + 2(s + t)) - 7(q + r - p^2) + p)/ m^3n^3 \nonumber \end{eqnarray}

The parameters \(p, q, r, s, t, v, u, a, b, w\) and \(x\) can be calculated from the following rank order probabilities:

\begin{eqnarray} p & = & P_{1,1}(0,0) \\ \nonumber q & = & P_{2,1}(0,0,1) \\ \nonumber r & = & P_{1,2}(0,1,1) \\ \nonumber s & = & P_{3,1}(0,0,0,1) \\ \nonumber t & = & P_{1,3}(0,1,1,1) \\ \nonumber v & = & P_{2,2}(0,0,1,1) \\ \nonumber u & = & v + (1/4) P_{2,2}(0,1,0,1) \\ \nonumber a & = & P_{4,1}(0,0,0,0,1) \\ \nonumber b & = & P_{1,4}(0,1,1,1,1) \\ \nonumber w & = & 2a + (2/3) P_{3,2}(0,0,1,0,1) + (1/6) P_{3,2}(0,1,0,0,1) \\ \nonumber x & = & 2b + (2/3) P_{2,3}(0,1,0,1,1) + (1/6) P_{2,3}(0,1,1,0,1) \\ \nonumber \end{eqnarray}

The rank order probabilities which are required for the calculation of the first 4 moments can be estimated from the sample as follows: Let \(U_i\) be the number of \(Y`s in the sample greater than `X_{(i)}\), where \(X_{(i)}\) is the ith ordered values of the \(X\) amongst themselves. Then the probabilities are calculated according to the following scheme :

\[P_{1,1}(0,1) = \frac{1}{mn} \sum_{i=1}^m U_i\]
\[P_{2,1}(0,0,1) = \frac{2}{mn(m-1)} \sum_{i=1}^m (i-1)U_i\]
\[P_{3,1}(0,0,0,1) = \frac{3}{mn(m-1)(m-2)} \sum_{i=1}^m (i-1)(i-2)U_i\]
\[P_{4,1}(0,0,0,0,1) = \frac{4}{mn(m-1)(m-2)(m-3)} \sum_{i=1}^m (i-1)(i-2)(i-3)U_i\]
\[P_{1,2}(0,1,1) = \frac{1}{mn(n-1)} \sum_{i=1}^m U_i(U_i-1)\]
\[P_{1,3}(0,1,1,1) = \frac{1}{mn(n-1)(n-2)} \sum_{i=1}^m U_i(U_i-1)(U_i-2)\]
\[P_{1,4}(0,1,1,1,1) = \frac{1}{mn(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)} \sum_{i=1}^m U_i(U_i-1)(U_i-2)(U_i-3)\]
\[P_{2,2}(0,0,1,1) = \frac{2}{mn(m-1)(n-1)} \sum_{i=1}^m (i-1)U_i(U_i-1)\]
\[P_{3,2}(0,0,0,1,1) = \frac{3}{mn(m-1)(m-2)(n-1)} \sum_{i=1}^m (i-1)(i-2)U_i(U_i-1)\]
\[P_{2,3}(0,0,1,1,1) = \frac{2}{mn(m-1)(n-1)(n-2)} \sum_{i=1}^m (i-1)U_i(U_i-1)(U_i-2)\]
\[P_{2,2}(0,1,0,1) = \frac{4}{mn(m-1)(n-1)} \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=i+1}^m (U_i - U_j) U_j\]
\[P_{3,2}(0,0,1,0,1) = \frac{12}{mn(m-1)(m-2)(n-1)} \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=i+1}^m (i-1)(U_i - U_j) U_j\]
\[P_{3,2}(0,1,0,0,1) = \frac{12}{mn(m-1)(m-2)(n-1)} \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=i+1}^m (i-j-1)(U_i - U_j) U_j\]
\[P_{2,3}(0,1,0,1,1) = \frac{6}{mn(m-1)(n-1)(n-2)} \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=i+1}^m (U_i - U_j) U_j (U_j-1)\]
\[P_{2,3}(0,1,0,1,1) = \frac{6}{mn(m-1)(n-1)(n-2)} \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=i+1}^m (U_i - U_j) U_j (U_j - 1)\]
\[P_{2,3}(0,1,1,0,1) = \frac{6}{mn(m-1)(n-1)(n-2)} \sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=i+1}^m (U_i - U_j) (U_i - U_j - 1) U_j\]

First 4 moments of Kendalls \(\tau\) in the general case (Sundrum)#

ctx.kendall_tau_nc_mu4(N)#

where ctx is dec, mpm, fpm, gmp or apm.

Returns an approximation to the noncentral distribution of Kendall’s \(\tau\), based on the first 4 moments.

Consider \(k\) pairs of random variables \((x_1,y_1),\ldots,(x_k,y_k)\). Let \(r_1,\ldots,r_k\) be a permutaton of \(1,\ldots,k\) and let \(s_1,\ldots,s_k\) be its reciprocal. We define as in Snow [530].

\(P(r_1,\ldots,r_k)\) = Pr[if \(x\)’s are ranked in order, ranks of corresponding \(y\)’s are \(r_1,\ldots,r_k\)].

\(P(r_1,\ldots,r_k)\) = Pr[if \(y\)’s are ranked in order, ranks of corresponding \(x\)’s are \(s_1,\ldots,s_k\)].

Let \(H(t) = 1\) if \(t>0\) and \(H(t)=0\) otherwise. Then

\[P(r_1,\ldots,r_k) = k! P \left[ \prod_{i=2}^k H(x_i - x_{i-1}) H(y_{s_i} - y_{s_{i-1}}) \right] = k! P \left[ \prod_{i=2}^k H(y_i - y_{i-1}) H(x_{r_i} - x_{r_{i-1}}) \right]\]

The rank order probabilities can then be estimated from the sample as

\[\hat{P}_{(12)} = \frac{1}{n(n-1)} \sum_{i \ne j} I\left[ (x_i-x_j)(y_i-y_j) > 0 \right]\]
\[\hat{P}_{(123)} = \frac{1}{n(n-1)(n-2)} \sum_{i \ne j_1 \ne j_2} I\left[ (x_i-x_{j_1})(y_i-y_{j_1}) (x_i-x_{j_2})(y_i-y_{j_2}) > 0 \right]\]

The first 4 moments of \(T_N\) are given as Sundrum [535]:

\[\mu_1 = p\]
\[\mu_2 = 2 [p(1 - p) + 2(k - p^2)(n - 2)] / [n (n - 1)]\]
\begin{eqnarray} \mu_3 & = & 4 [(t - 18kp + 10p^3) n^2 + (6k + 2u - 6p^2 - 5t + 72kp - 34p^3) n \\ \nonumber & + & (p + 9p^2 + 30p^3 - 12k - 4u + 6t - 72kp)] / [n (n - 1)]^2 \nonumber \end{eqnarray}
\begin{eqnarray} \mu_4 & = & 8 [6(k - p^2)2 n^4 + (6kp + 2y - 6p^3 - 16tp - 84k2 + 270kp^2 - 108p4) n^3 \\ \nonumber & + & (1.5p^2 + 6t + 2b - 126kp - 24up - 18y + 75p^3 + 120tp + 426k2 - 1446kp^2 + 505.5p4) n^2 \\ \nonumber & + & (14k + 12u - 15.5p^2 - 30t - 10b + 444kp + 96up + 52y - 213p^3 - 296tp - 924k2 \\ \nonumber & + & 2988kp^2 - 943.5p4)n \\ \nonumber & + & (p - 28k - 24u + 21p^2 + 36t + 12b - 432kp - 96up - 48y + 180p^3 + 240tp + 720k2 \\ \nonumber & - & 2160kp^2 + 630p4)] / [n (n - 1)]^3 \nonumber \end{eqnarray}

The parameters \(p, k, u, t, b, y\) can be calculated from the following rank order probabilities, writing \(P_{(1423)}\) for the probility that the ranks of the \(Y\) occur in this order when the \(X\) are sorted in ascending order:

\begin{eqnarray} p & = & P_{(12)} \\ \nonumber u & = & P_{(123)} \\ \nonumber k & = & P_{(123)} + \tfrac{1}{4}P_{(132)} \\ \nonumber t & = & 8(P_{(1234)} + P_{(1243))} + 6P_{(1342)} + 4P_{(1324)} + 2P_{(2143)} + P_{(2413)} + P_{(1432)} \\ \nonumber b & = & 15P_{(1234)} + 10P_{(1243)} + 5P_{(1324)} + 4P_{(1342)} + P_{(2143)} \\ \nonumber y & = & (160P_{(12453)} + 150(P_{(12435)} + P_{(12354))} + 125P_{(12345)} + 96P_{(21453)} + 90P_{(13254)} + 84P_{(13524)} \\ \nonumber &+& 80P_{(13425)} + 64P_{(13452)} + 45P_{(21354)} + 40P_{(12543)} + 32(P_{(23514)} + P_{(14352)} + P_{(13542)) } \\ \nonumber &+& 24P_{(21543)} + 22P_{(24153)} + 20P_{(14325)} + 18P_{(14523)} + 16P_{(25143)} + 12(P_{(24513)} + P_{(14532))} \\ \nonumber &+& 6(P_{(25314)} + P_{(15342))} + 4P_{(25413)} + 2(P_{(15432)} + P_{(35142)}))/5 \nonumber \end{eqnarray}

When \((x_i,y_i)\), \(i=1,2,\ldots,k\), are normal variables with correlation \(\rho\), then closed form expressions exist for \(p, k\) and \(u\) (writing \(a = \arcsin\left(\rho\right)/\pi\) and \(b = 2\arcsin\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\rho\right)/\pi\):

\begin{eqnarray} p & = & \tfrac{1}{2} + a \\ \nonumber k & = & \tfrac{1}{4} (\tfrac{10}{9} + 4a + 4a^2 - b^2) \\ \nonumber u & = & \tfrac{3}{8} (\tfrac{4}{9} + 4a -2b + 4a^2 - b^2) \nonumber \end{eqnarray}

The parameters \(t, b\) and \(y\) have to be evaluated by numerical intergration of the rank order probabilities
